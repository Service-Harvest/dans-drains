Common Disposal Problems

Disposals usually fail in familiar ways:

  • It hums but will not turn.
  • It is completely dead.
  • It leaks from the bottom or the connections.
  • It drains slowly or backs up.

A hum without spinning usually means a jam, which is often a quick fix.

What Causes Disposal Problems

Jams from bones, fibrous scraps, or utensils are the most common. Leaks come from worn seals or loose connections, and a dead unit may just need a reset or have reached the end of its life.

If the sink drain itself is slow, the disposal may not be the culprit — that can be a clogged kitchen drain line.

How We Repair a Disposal

We clear jams, reset or repair the motor where possible, and reseal or tighten leaking connections. If the unit is burned out or badly corroded, we replace it and connect the new one cleanly to the drain.

We test it thoroughly before finishing so it runs quiet and drains well.

Repair or Replace?

Jams and minor leaks are usually simple repairs. Replacement makes sense when the motor is burned out or the unit is old and corroded. A new disposal is inexpensive and quick to install.

Either way, we handle disposals as a small job and connect them properly to your kitchen sink plumbing. For what belongs in a disposal and what does not, the Family Handyman garbage disposal do's and don'ts is a handy reference.

When to Call a Pro

Call us when your disposal hums, leaks, or quits. Never put your hand inside a disposal to clear a jam — that is exactly the kind of quick, safe fix we handle.

Leaks under the sink deserve prompt attention to avoid cabinet damage.

Frequently Asked Questions

My disposal hums but does not spin — what is wrong?

That usually means it is jammed. There is often a simple fix, but never reach inside the unit. We clear jams safely and get it running.

Why is my disposal leaking?

Leaks usually come from worn seals or loose drain connections. We find the source and reseal or replace as needed.

Should I repair or replace my disposal?

Jams and minor leaks are usually worth repairing. A burned-out motor or an old, corroded unit is better replaced, since a new one is inexpensive.

Can a disposal cause a slow drain?

Sometimes the disposal is fine and the drain line beyond it is clogged. We check both so we fix the real cause.

What should I avoid putting down my disposal?

Avoid bones, fibrous scraps like celery, grease, and hard items. These cause most of the jams and clogs we see.
