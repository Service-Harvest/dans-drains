What to Do Right Now

If water is actively flowing where it should not be, the first step is to stop it. For most home emergencies:

  • Shut off the fixture's valve if there is one, or the main water shutoff for the whole house.
  • For a water heater leak, turn off its supply and, for gas units, the gas.
  • Move valuables away from the water and stay clear of any outlet the water has reached.
  • Call us — we will talk you through it and head your way.

You do not have to figure it out alone. We will stay on the phone and help.

Emergencies We Handle

We respond to the plumbing problems that cannot wait: a pipe that has burst and is flooding a room, a major supply leak, a sewer backing up into the house, a failed water heater, and no-water situations.

How We Respond to an Emergency

When you call, we help you stop the water first, then head out as fast as we can. On arrival, we find the source, stop the leak, and stabilize the situation before doing anything else.

Then we explain what happened and what the repair involves, and give you a price before we start — even under pressure, you are never handed a surprise bill afterward.

Upfront Pricing, Even in a Crisis

An emergency is stressful enough without worrying about being taken advantage of. We give you an honest, upfront price for the emergency repair before we begin the work.

Acting fast also limits the damage. The FEMA guidance on limiting home water damage reinforces why stopping the water quickly matters, and it is exactly what we help you do.

Preventing the Next Emergency

Once the crisis is handled, we will point out what caused it and how to avoid a repeat — insulating vulnerable pipes, replacing a tired shutoff, or watching an aging water heater.

Simple safeguards like early-warning leak sensors placed in risky spots can catch the next problem before it floods a room.

Frequently Asked Questions

How fast can you get here?

We keep room in the schedule for emergencies and get to you as quickly as we can. When you call, we will give you an honest time frame and help you stop the water in the meantime.

Where is my main water shutoff?

It is usually where the water line enters the house — often in the basement, a utility area, or near the water meter. If you cannot find it, call us and we will help you locate it on the phone.

Do you charge more for emergency calls?

We give you an upfront price before any work begins, even in an emergency. You will know the cost before we start, not after.

My basement is flooding — what should I do first?

Shut off the water at the source or the main, stay away from any outlets the water has reached, and call us right away. We will guide you and get moving.

Can a burst pipe wait until morning?

No. A burst pipe can release a lot of water fast and cause serious damage. Shut off the water and call us as soon as it happens.
