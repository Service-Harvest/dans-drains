How We Install a Water Heater

We start by understanding how your household actually uses hot water — how many people, how many bathrooms, and whether you ever run out. Then we recommend a unit sized to match, drain and disconnect the old heater, and install the new one with proper connections, venting, and safety fittings.

Before we leave, we test it, check for leaks, and make sure it is heating the way it should. You get a clean install and a straight answer about how to get the most life out of it.

Choosing the Right Water Heater

The big choice is usually tank versus tankless. A standard tank is simpler and cheaper up front. A tankless unit that heats water on demand costs more but saves space and can lower energy use. If you heat with gas, a properly vented gas water heater is often the most economical to run.

We lay out the real trade-offs for your home and budget instead of steering you to the priciest option.

What Affects the Cost

A few things drive the price of a water heater install:

  • Tank versus tankless, and the capacity you need.
  • Whether the new unit fits the existing space and connections.
  • Any venting, gas, or electrical updates the new unit requires.
  • Code-required safety items like an expansion tank or new shutoff.

We give you the full price up front so there are no surprises after the work is done.

Water Heaters in Westchester Homes

Many homes here have water heaters tucked into finished or semi-finished basements, and some have hard water that shortens a heater's life by building up scale inside the tank. We take both into account when we size and place your unit.

For efficiency comparisons, the ENERGY STAR water heater information is a helpful reference, and we translate those ratings into what actually fits your home.

When to Call a Pro

Water heater installation involves gas or electrical connections, venting, and water under pressure — not a safe do-it-yourself job. Call us when your heater is over ten years old, leaking, or no longer keeping up.

If your current unit is failing but not dead yet, our water heater repair service can sometimes buy you time to plan the replacement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a water heater installation take?

A straightforward tank replacement is often done in a few hours. A tankless conversion or a job that needs new venting or gas work takes longer, and we will give you a realistic time frame up front.

How long should a water heater last?

A typical tank heater lasts around 8 to 12 years, though hard water and heavy use can shorten that. Tankless units often last longer with proper maintenance.

Should I switch to tankless?

Tankless is great for endless hot water and space savings, but it costs more up front and sometimes needs gas or venting upgrades. We will tell you honestly whether it pays off for your home.

Can you install a heater I bought myself?

Often, yes, though we will check that it is the right size and type for your home first. We want the install to actually solve your hot-water problem, not just swap a box.

Do I need an expansion tank?

In many cases, local code requires one to handle pressure changes safely. We include any code-required items in the upfront price rather than surprising you later.
