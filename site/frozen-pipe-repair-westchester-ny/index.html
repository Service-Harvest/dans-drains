Signs a Pipe Is Frozen

Catching a freeze before it bursts saves a lot of trouble. Watch for:

  • No water or just a trickle from a faucet on a cold day.
  • Frost visible on an exposed pipe.
  • A bulging section of pipe.
  • Strange smells from a faucet or drain, a sign of a blockage.

If a pipe is frozen, it is under stress and can split as it thaws, so act carefully.

How to Thaw a Pipe Safely

Never use an open flame to thaw a pipe — it is a fire risk and can crack the pipe. Gentle warmth and keeping the faucet open so melting water can escape is the safe approach.

If you cannot reach the frozen section, or you suspect it has already cracked, stop and call us before it becomes a full burst-pipe emergency.

How We Handle a Frozen Pipe

We locate the frozen section, thaw it safely, and check for cracks the freeze may have caused. If the pipe was damaged, we repair or replace the affected section on the spot.

Then we look for why it froze — an uninsulated run, a cold draft — so we can help you prevent a repeat.

Freeze Risk in Westchester Homes

Our winters get cold enough to freeze pipes in crawl spaces, exterior walls, garages, and unheated basements. Homes with older insulation or exposed runs are especially at risk during a hard freeze.

The Consumer Reports guide to preventing frozen pipes offers practical steps, and we handle the repairs and safeguards side.

Preventing Frozen Pipes

Insulating vulnerable pipes, sealing cold drafts, keeping the heat on during cold snaps, and letting a faucet drip on the coldest nights all reduce freeze risk. We will point out which pipes in your home need protecting.

If a freeze has already stressed your plumbing, a quick check and repair of the affected pipe prevents a surprise later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I thaw a frozen pipe myself?

Gentle warmth with the faucet open can work for an accessible pipe, but never use an open flame. If you cannot reach it or suspect a crack, call us before it bursts.

Will a frozen pipe always burst?

Not always, but it is under stress and can split as it thaws. It is safest to thaw it carefully and check for damage.

Which pipes are most likely to freeze?

Pipes in unheated areas — crawl spaces, exterior walls, garages, and basements — and any exposed or poorly insulated runs.

How can I prevent frozen pipes?

Insulate at-risk pipes, seal drafts, keep the heat on during cold snaps, and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. We will help identify your risk spots.

What should I do if no water comes out on a cold morning?

You may have a frozen pipe. Keep the faucet open, apply gentle warmth if you can safely reach the pipe, and call us if it does not clear or you suspect damage.
