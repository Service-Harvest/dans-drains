What Low Pressure Looks Like

Low pressure shows up as a weak shower, a slow-filling tub, or fixtures that trickle instead of flow. The key question is whether it affects one fixture or the whole house.

  • One fixture: often a clogged aerator or a local supply issue.
  • The whole house: usually a pressure regulator, a failing line, or corrosion.

Which it is tells us where to look.

Common Causes of Low Pressure

Frequent culprits include mineral buildup in fixtures, corroded or narrowing pipe, a failing pressure regulator, a partially closed valve, or a hidden leak bleeding off pressure.

If corrosion in old pipe is the cause, the lasting fix may be a replacement of the affected pipe rather than a temporary patch.

How We Restore Pressure

We test pressure at key points to isolate the cause, then fix it — cleaning or replacing a clogged fixture, adjusting or replacing a regulator, or addressing a failing line. We confirm the pressure is back where it should be before we finish.

If a hidden leak is quietly stealing pressure, our leak-finding service pinpoints it.

Pressure Issues in Older Homes

Older homes in this area with aging galvanized or mixed pipe often lose pressure as the pipe corrodes and narrows inside. What feels like a fixture problem is sometimes a whole-house pipe issue.

Comfortable, efficient water use depends on proper pressure, something the EPA WaterSense showerhead information touches on, and we get yours back to where it should be.

When to Call a Pro

Call us when pressure drops across the house, a fix at one fixture does not help, or pressure keeps getting worse. We find the actual source instead of masking it.

If pressure loss comes with discolored water, it may point to the main line — our service line evaluation can check.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is my water pressure low in the whole house?

Whole-house low pressure usually points to a failing pressure regulator, corroded pipe, a partly closed main valve, or a hidden leak. We test to find which.

Only one faucet is weak — what is that?

A single weak fixture is often a clogged aerator or a local supply issue, which is usually a quick fix. We confirm before doing more.

Can a leak cause low pressure?

Yes. A hidden leak bleeds off water and pressure. If we suspect one, we locate it as part of solving the pressure problem.

Can old pipes cause low pressure?

Definitely. Corroded galvanized or aging pipe narrows inside over time, restricting flow. Replacing it restores pressure for good.

Is low pressure something I can fix myself?

Cleaning a fixture aerator is a fine DIY step. If that does not help or the whole house is affected, it is worth having us find the real source.
